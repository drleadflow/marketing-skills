# Brand voice

You'll finish with a voice guide in section 6 of `.agents/business-context.md`: three voice traits with "this, not that" examples, the words and phrases that are yours, the ones that are banned, the sentence shapes your best-performing posts share, what killed reach, how the voice flexes by channel, and three before-and-after rewrites. Every writing skill in this repo reads it, so drafts sound like the owner on a good day.

The guide is built from the owner's real words and real results, not from adjectives they wish described them. "Friendly and professional" describes everyone. "Short lines, one idea each, names the thing nobody says, never sells in the last line" describes someone.

## Before you start

- Read `.agents/business-context.md` and `.agents/proof-ledger.md` if they exist. Section 3 (best client, in their words) tells you who the voice is talking to. If business-context is missing, offer to run **business-context** first, or ask: What do you sell? Who to? Where do you publish most?
- Ask for samples, in this order of value:
  1. **Their 5 best-performing posts or emails** (most saves, shares, replies or sales), and **3 that flopped**. Ask for the numbers if they have them.
  2. **How they talk**: a sales call transcript, a voice memo, a podcast or video transcript. Spoken words are the purest sample.
  3. **Messages they wrote to customers** that got a warm reply.
  4. Two or three creators or brands they'd like to sound closer to, and one they never want to sound like.
- Fewer than 5 samples total: say the guide will be a first draft and should be redone after 20 more pieces ship.

## How to do it

### 1. Separate their voice from their polish

Sort the samples into three piles:

- **Spoken** (transcripts, voice notes): their natural rhythm and words.
- **Written winners**: what their audience rewards.
- **Written flops and polished pieces** (agency-written, over-edited): usually what to avoid.

Where spoken and winners agree, that's the voice. Where the polished pieces differ from both, that's the "not that".

### 2. Measure, don't guess

For each pile, note:

| Feature | What to record |
|---|---|
| Sentence length | Typical words per sentence; share of sentences under 8 words |
| Line shape | One idea per line, or paragraphs? Fragments? |
| Openings | How the first line works (a claim, a question, a scene, a number, a callout) |
| Endings | Land on a value line, a question, or a sales ask? |
| Person | I / we / you; do they talk to one person or a crowd? |
| Repeated words and phrases | Anything that shows up 3 or more times |
| Signature moves | Structures they reuse (normalize, reject, reframe; two options; a list as rhythm) |
| Stance | Peer to peer, teacher to student, friend, expert to novice |
| What's never there | Hype words, emojis, jargon, exclamation marks |

Then compare winners against flops. The differences are your evidence. A typical finding: winners name a painful truth in short lines and land on one idea; flops are polished paragraphs, stacked stats and a hard sell at the end.

### 3. Write three voice traits

Each trait gets a one-line definition and a "this, not that" pair from their own material.

```markdown
**Straight.** Say the thing in the fewest words, then stop.
- This: "Most of your leads never got a call. That's the whole problem."
- Not that: "Many businesses may find that their lead follow-up processes present opportunities for improvement."
```

Traits must be checkable. "Authentic" can't be checked. "No sentence over 20 words" can.

### 4. Build the word lists

- **Our words**: 8 to 15 words and phrases from the samples that feel like them. Include the customer's words they've adopted.
- **Never words**: hype (revolutionary, game-changer, unlock, supercharge, skyrocket, delve), filler (just, really, very, basically), corporate (streamline, solutions, synergy), anything the owner hates, and any word their industry may not use (see section 10, Constraints).
- **Swaps**: for each never word they tend to slip into, the plain replacement.

### 5. Name the shapes that work

From the winners, write 3 to 5 reusable structures with a skeleton each, for example:

```
Normalize, reject, reframe:
  "Everyone says X." / "X is common. It isn't normal." / "Here's what's going on."
Two options:
  "You can do A, and keep getting B. Or you can do C."
```

And list what killed reach in the flops, plainly: "polished paragraphs", "three stats in a row", "two calls to action".

### 6. Set the channel dials

The voice stays the same; the volume changes. Fill this table:

| Channel | Length | Formality | Selling | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Short video | | | | |
| Captions and posts | | | | |
| Email | | | | |
| DMs and texts | | | | |
| Sales page | | | | |
| Ads | | | | |

DMs and texts should read like a person typing on a phone: short, lowercase is fine if that's how they text, one question at a time.

### 7. Prove it with three rewrites

Take three real pieces of generic copy (theirs, or a competitor's) and rewrite each in the voice. Show before and after side by side. This is the part owners read, and the part future drafts copy.

### 8. Write it down

Write section 6 of `.agents/business-context.md` with these subsections, and append a changelog line:

```markdown
## 6. Voice

### Traits
### Our words
### Never words and swaps
### Shapes that work
### What kills it
### Channel dials
### Rewrites
### Samples
(3 to 5 short real lines, labeled with where they came from)
```

If section 6 already exists, show the old and new side by side and get a yes before replacing it.

## Check it

Run a blind test before showing the guide: write one new short post with the guide, mix it with two of the owner's real winners, and check whether the new one stands out. Then score:

| # | Check | Pass when |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Evidence | Every trait and shape cites real samples |
| 2 | Checkable | Each trait can be checked on a draft (a length, a word, a structure), not just felt |
| 3 | This, not that | Each trait has a pair drawn from real or realistic copy |
| 4 | Word lists | Our words, never words and swaps all filled |
| 5 | Winners vs flops | The guide states what separates them |
| 6 | Channel dials | Every channel the business uses has a row |
| 7 | Rewrites | Three before-and-after pairs |
| 8 | Customer words | Our words include phrases customers use |
| 9 | Constraints | Industry-banned words are in the never list |
| 10 | Clean | No hype words, no em or en dashes in the guide itself |

## Rules

- Build from the owner's real words. Never invent a sample and present it as theirs; label any example you wrote as "example".
- Never copy another creator's lines or signature phrases into the guide. Learn the shape, leave the words.
- The voice never overrides the proof rules: no claim gets stronger because it sounds better. Check the proof ledger.
- Never write the voice so it talks down to the customer or shames them.
- Keep any customer quotes in samples free of names and identifying details.
- Re-run after every 20 to 30 published pieces, or when results drop, using the new winners.

## Ask your audience

Ask 3 to 5 customers, in a message or on a call:

1. How would you describe us to a friend, in one sentence?
2. What's something we said that stuck with you?
3. What made you trust us enough to reach out?
4. What almost stopped you?
5. Is there anything we say that sounds like every other company?

Add the phrases they use to "Our words".

## Examples

A short voice guide for a fictional business coach:

```markdown
### Traits
**Straight.** Fewest words, then stop. Under 15 words a sentence on average.
- This: "Your calendar isn't empty because of the algorithm."
- Not that: "In today's landscape, many factors influence calendar utilization."
**Peer, not guru.** Talks like an owner who's been there, never from a stage.
- This: "I made this mistake for two years."
- Not that: "Successful entrepreneurs know that..."
**One idea.** Every post lands on one line worth saving.

### Our words
"the numbers on Monday", "one offer", "own it", "the follow-up is the business"

### Never words and swaps
unlock → get · leverage → use · solutions → (name the thing) · journey → (cut)

### What kills it
Three stats in a row. Paragraphs over 4 lines. Two asks at the end.
```
