# No-show recovery script

A defined conversation, not an assumption about why someone didn't show. Confirm non-attendance against the real calendar first; see SKILL.md step 6.

## Shape

1. Acknowledge, don't accuse. "We had you down for [named appointment] at [time] today and didn't get to connect."
2. Open the door, don't close it. Ask one question rather than stating a conclusion.
3. Offer the real next option from the live calendar, not a vague "let us know."
4. Stop after a defined number of attempts; don't turn this into an open-ended sequence.

## Fictional business coach example

"Hey [name], we had you down for the offer audit call at 2pm today and missed you. Totally understand if the timing didn't work out. Want me to grab you a new slot, or is now not the right moment?"

## Fictional home-services company example

"Hi [name], our tech was at [address] for the scheduled diagnostic at [time] and wasn't able to get in. No charge for the missed visit. Want to grab a new window this week, or should we hold off for now?"

## Fictional med spa example

"Hi [name], we had you down for your consult at [time] today and weren't able to connect. If something came up, totally understandable. Would you like help finding a new time, or would you rather we check back with you another day?"

## Rules for this message

- Never imply blame or guilt.
- Never offer a discount as the first response to a no-show; that trains people to skip and get a better deal.
- If a deposit was taken, state what happens to it plainly and match whatever the business's actual published policy says (credited, forfeited, or extended), never improvised in the moment.
- Stop after 2 to 3 attempts and log the record as closed rather than running an indefinite re-engagement loop.
