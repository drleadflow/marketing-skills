# Chokepoint diagnostics

For the chokepoint you picked: read the symptoms, ask the questions 3 at a time, check the evidence, then match the cause to a fix and a skill. Tag each finding confirmed (seen in data, recordings or threads) or suspected (owner's account only).

---

## 1. Offer

**Symptoms:** prospects ask "how much?" and disappear; sales calls end on price; discounts are the main lever; busy but not profitable; different prices in different places; people can't say what they're buying.

**Questions**
1. What exactly does a new customer buy first, and what is it called?
2. What does it cost you to deliver one, including the time of whoever delivers it?
3. Is the price, what's included and the terms the same on the site, the ads, the scripts and the invoice? Show me each.
4. When you lose a sale, what do people say, in their words?
5. Could a customer compare this line by line with a competitor's price list?
6. Does the first purchase make money on its own, or only if they buy again?
7. What's the one thing a buyer keeps that isn't money off?

**Evidence to check:** three places the price appears; the last 10 lost-sale notes; the delivery cost sheet.

| Cause | Fix | Skill |
|---|---|---|
| Two or more versions live | One approved version of price, inclusions and terms, then update every surface | offer-builder |
| Sold as a commodity (per unit, per hour) | Package around the result and the customer's words, not the units | offer-builder |
| Margin under the floor | Reprice up from delivery cost, or cut delivery cost; never price below cost | offer-ladder-pricing |
| No clear entry step | Name the first visit or call, make it worth paying for on its own | offer-ladder-pricing |
| Built for everyone | Narrow to the best client and their trigger moment | best-client-profile, positioning |
| Hard to explain | One page the prospect can read before the call | one-page-offer |

## 2. Attention

**Symptoms:** downstream rates are healthy but there aren't enough leads; content gets no reach; ad costs climb; one ad carries everything and is wearing out.

**Questions**
1. Where did your last 20 customers first hear of you?
2. How many pieces of content or ads ran each week for the last 8 weeks?
3. Who is the content for? Would your best client stop scrolling for it?
4. What's your cost per lead, and what's the most you can pay (the ceiling from the backward math)?
5. How much of your lead flow comes from one ad, post or channel?
6. When you post your best-performing topic, what is it about?

**Evidence to check:** ad account by creative; top 10 posts by saves and shares; lead source field.

| Cause | Fix | Skill |
|---|---|---|
| Inconsistent output | A weekly content rhythm the owner can sustain | content-pillars, short-form-script |
| Content for peers, not buyers | Topics from the buyer's questions and trigger moments | content-pillars, outlier-research |
| Weak openings | New hooks on proven topics | hook-writer |
| One creative carrying the account | Build the next 3 angles before it wears out | ad-angles-and-copy, ad-creative-brief |
| Paid account wasting spend | Audit structure, tracking and spend | ad-account-audit |
| Not found in search or AI answers | Answer the questions buyers search | seo-article, ai-search-visibility |

## 3. Leads

**Symptoms:** plenty of clicks or views, few contacts; "the leads are garbage"; leads sit for hours; nobody knows how many leads never got called; DMs answered days later.

**Questions**
1. When a lead comes in at 10am on a Tuesday, what happens, minute by minute? Who gets notified?
2. What about 8pm on a Saturday?
3. Of last month's leads, how many got a real reply or call at all? (Count it, don't estimate.)
4. Is a "thanks, we got it" auto-reply being counted as a response?
5. What does the page or form ask for before someone can raise their hand?
6. Does every lead source land in one place, or do some live in a DM inbox nobody owns?
7. Which lead sources book best once contacted?

**Evidence to check:** CRM timestamps (lead created vs first human touch); 10 lead threads read end to end; the form and page on a phone.

| Cause | Fix | Skill |
|---|---|---|
| Slow first reply | A reply standard the team can staff, plus an instant text that asks one question | speed-to-lead |
| Leads never worked (coverage gap) | One owner per queue, a same-day list for anyone not reached | speed-to-lead |
| Leads stuck in DMs | Route DMs into the same pipeline with an owner | comment-to-dm, speed-to-lead |
| Page doesn't convert | One offer, one action, price and terms explained before asking for details | landing-page |
| Nothing to raise a hand for | A useful free resource tied to the offer | lead-magnet, quiz-funnel |
| "Garbage leads" | Check speed and coverage first; then qualifying questions on the form | speed-to-lead, landing-page |

"The leads are bad" is usually a speed or coverage problem wearing a disguise. Check the threads before changing the ads.

## 4. Appointments

**Symptoms:** lots of conversations, few bookings; "I'll get back to you"; bookings that don't show; no one knows the show rate.

**Questions**
1. When a lead says "yes, I'm interested", what's the exact next message? Does it offer two real times?
2. How many follow-ups does a lead who goes quiet get, over how many days, and when does it stop?
3. Are shows and no-shows marked on the calendar?
4. What happens between booking and the appointment? Read me the reminders.
5. Does anything automatically cancel or release a booking? List every rule.
6. Is a deposit or fee taken? Where is it explained, and does it differ by channel?
7. If an AI or a bot is involved, when does a human take over, and how fast?

**Evidence to check:** 10 threads that didn't book; the calendar's status field for last month; every automation that touches a booking.

| Cause | Fix | Skill |
|---|---|---|
| No concrete time offered | Offer two real slots the moment intent shows | setter-script |
| Follow-up stops after one try | A dated sequence with a set end | follow-up-sequence |
| Quiet after booking | Confirmation, a reason to come, reminders that assume they're coming | show-rate |
| Auto-cancel rules | Inventory every rule; no automation cancels a booked customer without human review | show-rate |
| Text-only conversations stall | Add voice notes or a call at the stall point | voice-note-dms |
| Bot runs with no handoff | Clear handoff rules, a human within 15 minutes, guardrails | ai-setter-guardrails |
| Deposit is the objection on one channel | Test the deposit per channel, not as a global rule | show-rate, offer-builder |

## 5. Closed

**Symptoms:** people show up and don't buy; "I need to think about it"; discounts on the call; close rate swings by who runs the call; no follow-up after the call.

**Questions**
1. Walk me through your call or consult. What happens in the first 5 minutes?
2. Do you ask what they want before you explain what you sell?
3. Do you make one recommendation and then ask for the decision?
4. What are the top 3 reasons people don't buy, in their words?
5. What happens to someone who says "let me think about it"? Who follows up, when?
6. How is payment taken? Can they pay on the call? Are terms in writing before they pay?
7. Does close rate differ by person? By lead source?

**Evidence to check:** 3 recorded calls (with consent), one won, two lost; the proposal or quote; the pending list.

| Cause | Fix | Skill |
|---|---|---|
| No structure; pitching before listening | A call flow that starts with their goal in their words | sales-call-playbook |
| Same objections, no answers | A written response to each top objection | objection-handling |
| Inconsistent between people | Grade calls on the process, practice the weak step | call-grader |
| No decision asked for | One recommendation, then a direct ask | sales-call-playbook |
| Payment friction, vague terms | Clear proposal, terms and payment link at the decision moment | proposal-and-payment |
| No follow-up on undecided | Every undecided prospect gets an owner and a dated next step | sales-call-playbook |

## 6. Fulfillment

**Symptoms:** refunds, complaints or quiet churn; customers don't finish; delivery depends on the owner; quality varies by who delivers; few referrals despite many customers.

**Questions**
1. What happens in a new customer's first 7 days? Who contacts them?
2. What's the first result they should feel, and by when?
3. What do the last 3 unhappy customers have in common?
4. Is delivery written down, or in someone's head?
5. Does quality differ by who delivers? Do you track results per person?
6. What's the one thing customers most often complain about?

**Evidence to check:** refund and complaint log; the onboarding messages; results split by who delivered.

| Cause | Fix | Skill |
|---|---|---|
| Rough start | A first-week plan with a quick first win | client-onboarding |
| Delivery lives in one head | Written process for each repeated job | sop-generator |
| Customers stuck between sessions | A helper that answers how-to questions in the business's words | client-assistant |
| Promise bigger than delivery | Rewrite the promise to what you control | offer-builder |

Fulfillment gates Retention and Referrals. Don't run a referral push on top of a red Fulfillment score.

## 7. Retention

**Symptoms:** every month starts from zero; a big past-customer list nobody contacts; no rebooking; memberships that don't stick.

**Questions**
1. How many past customers are in your system, and when did each last buy?
2. Is the next visit or purchase booked before the customer leaves? Who does it?
3. Is there a natural interval when customers need you again? Do you remind them?
4. When did the past-customer list last hear from you, and with what?
5. Do you have a recurring option? What do members get, and do they use it?
6. Are people already booked or in active service kept out of promotional messages?

**Evidence to check:** last-purchase date distribution; rebook rate at the end of a service; the last 5 messages sent to past customers.

| Cause | Fix | Skill |
|---|---|---|
| Silent database | A reactivation campaign to past leads and customers, in batches the team can handle | database-reactivation |
| No rebooking moment | Book the next one before they leave, phrased as when, not if | repeat-purchase |
| No reason to stay | A membership with real value, honest terms, easy cancellation | membership-design |
| Nothing between purchases | A light, useful touch on a set rhythm | nurture-cadence |

## 8. Referrals

**Symptoms:** happy customers but few introductions; few reviews; no idea where new customers came from.

**Questions**
1. Do you ask "how did you hear about us?" at every booking?
2. When is the referral ask made, by whom, and is it the same every time?
3. What does a referrer get, and how does it compare with what a new customer costs you to acquire?
4. When do you ask for a review, and do you ask everyone at that point?
5. Is there a story from a happy customer you're allowed to share?

**Evidence to check:** referral source field; review dates against job dates; the proof ledger.

| Cause | Fix | Skill |
|---|---|---|
| No consistent ask | A named ask at the moment results are visible | referral-program |
| Incentive too small next to acquisition cost | Set the reward against what a new customer costs to acquire, within your industry's rules | referral-program |
| Few reviews | Ask every customer at the same point; never incentivize or filter | review-engine |
| No shareable stories | Turn approved results into case studies | case-study |
