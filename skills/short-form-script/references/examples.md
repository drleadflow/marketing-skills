# Worked example: 5:5:3 script set (fictional business coach)

Business: Avery Lane Coaching (fictional). Topic: why "more leads" doesn't fix a slow coaching business. Free resource (exists): a 5-question "Follow-Up Audit" quiz.

## 5 hooks (different categories)

1. **Withheld truth**: "Most coaches think their problem is leads. It's never leads."
2. **Before-you-do-X**: "Before you spend another dollar on ads, give me 60 seconds."
3. **Contrarian**: "I turned down a lead gen project last month. Here's why."
4. **Identity**: "If you've got plenty of leads and still a slow month, this is for you."
5. **Specific stake**: "[NEEDS PROOF: a real, sourced statistic on how much revenue a slow follow-up window costs, or drop the number and open on the mechanism instead]"

## 1 body (pairs with any hook above, under 160 words)

"Here's what I see over and over. Someone gets a strong month of leads, closes almost none of them, and decides the problem is the ads. It's usually not. The leak is almost always the same: a lead goes 3 or 4 days without a reply. By then, most people have already found someone else or talked themselves out of it. I don't think more leads fixes a slow business. I think a tighter follow-up window does. It's not glamorous. It won't make a good ad. But it's the thing that moves the number. If you want to see where your own gap is, I built a short audit. Takes less than 2 minutes."

## 3 CTAs

- **Direct**: "Book a free 20-minute call and we'll find your gap together. Link in bio."
- **Indirect**: "Take the 2-minute Follow-Up Audit (link in bio) and see your gap before we ever talk."
- **Comment trigger**: "Comment AUDIT and I'll send you the link."

## Anti-slop gate result (first draft, before the fix below)

```
Script: "More Leads Doesn't Fix It" (full set)
Result: FAIL(2)

Flagged lines:
1. UNEARNED CLAIM / check 6: "The average coach loses 60% of leads to a follow-up gap, not a traffic gap."
 Why it fails: a specific percentage with no named source. Reads as a real statistic; isn't one yet.
 Fix: either source a real number from a study or the business's own CRM data, or drop the
 number and state the mechanism plainly: "[NEEDS PROOF]" placeholder above.
2. INVENTED-FACT / check 11: "I pulled the data on one client's funnel and found the real leak."
 Why it fails: names a specific unverified event (a specific client, a specific data pull) that
 didn't happen. This is the auto-fail check; a vivid detail doesn't make it true.
 Fix: rewrite as a pattern observed across clients generally ("here's what I see over and over"),
 not a single invented incident.
```
