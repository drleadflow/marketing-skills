# Hook library: 13 categories, ordered by virality ceiling

Fill every `[bracket]` with a real specific. A generic fill kills the hook.

## 1. The Receipt: proof, numbers, results, highest ceiling

1. [N] [things] later, only [smaller N] of them actually worked.
2. [Time period] of [doing a specific thing], and this is what happened.
3. The real numbers behind [big accomplishment], broken into [N] lessons.
4. We checked [N] [things] by hand. Here's what [N] of them had in common.
5. [Past situation] to [result], and it took [time], not [longer assumed time].
6. The proof that [a claim most people doubt] is real.

> Fictional example (business coach): "30 coaching calls later, only 4 had an actual close in them."

## 2. Contrarian / myth-buster: highest ceiling, polarizing

1. [Common belief] is the reason this keeps not working.
2. [Common practice] stopped helping anyone around the time [a real shift happened].
3. [Common advice] sounds right and does the opposite of what people think.
4. An unpopular take: [the contrarian position], and here's why it holds up.
5. Everything circulating about [topic] skips the one part that matters.

> Fictional example (med spa): "Most people think a membership means a lower standard of care. It's the opposite: it's how we plan around you instead of around a one-off visit."

## 3. Negative frame / mistake callout

1. The [N] ways [task] quietly goes wrong, and nobody mentions them.
2. Whoever told you to [common action] left out the part that matters.
3. [Task] keeps failing for a reason that has nothing to do with effort.
4. The version of [task] everyone was taught is the slow version.

> Fictional example (home services): "Stop accepting the lowest quote. Here's what it's hiding."

## 4. Stolen lessons / steal this

1. [Specific thing] got copied exactly, and the result surprised even us.
2. [Famous brand/person]'s [specific tactic], tried on a much smaller budget: [outcome].
3. The [industry] move almost nobody outside the industry knows about.

## 5. Curiosity gap / open loop

1. What nobody mentions about [topic], until it's too late to matter.
2. The part of [situation] that stayed hidden until someone finally asked.
3. What I wish someone had told me before [starting a specific thing].

## 6. Listicle / number hook

1. [N] things about [niche] that took far too long to learn.
2. [N] signs it's already time to [take an action].
3. [N] steps to [outcome] that almost everyone skips on the way there.

## 7. Secret / insider

1. The part of working in [industry] nobody explains to outsiders.
2. The one overlooked piece that actually drives [outcome].

## 8. Audience callout / pattern interrupt

1. [Specific group], this one's worth the extra three seconds.
2. [N]% of [niche] gets this backwards, and most never find out.
3. If you're [specific situation], keep reading.

## 9. Question hook

1. Did you know [a shocking, specific fact]?
2. Still [doing a common thing] the hard way?
3. Ever notice how [pain point] always shows up at the worst time?

## 10. Transformation / before-after / story

1. What [project] looked like before, and what changed it.
2. [Achieved result], without [the thing everyone assumes is required].
3. [Time period] into [doing a specific thing], this is what shifted.

## 11. Speed / effortless how-to

1. [Goal], done in [a surprisingly short time].
2. [Goal], without [the usual obstacle] getting in the way.

## 12. Urgency / stop-scroll

Use sparingly. Overuse trains the audience to tune it out entirely.

1. Worth pausing for if [specific benefit] matters to you right now.
2. A sign worth taking seriously: [specific, real reason to act].

## 13. Confession / vulnerable

1. Not something I'd normally say out loud, but [admission].
2. Used to [do something a specific wrong way]. Here's what replaced it.

## Writing rules for every hook

- A detail beats a category. Swap out any placeholder for a real number, a real name, or a real moment before it leaves your hands; an unfilled bracket is a hook that was never finished.
- Lead with whatever word carries the surprise. If the number or the reversal is buried in the middle of the sentence, move it to the front.
- Test it in isolation: would this line make sense and still land if someone saw only it, with no caption underneath? If it needs the next sentence to mean anything, it's not pulling its own weight yet.
- Strip padding words on the read-through: really, very, just, basically. They add length, not meaning.
- Skip the openers a reader has seen a thousand times from a bot: a scene-setting "picture this," a breathless "let me tell you," a scope-everything "in today's world."
- Each hook carries exactly one idea. Two ideas crammed into one line means neither one lands; cut it to one and save the second for the next post.
- Rotate categories across a batch. A feed where every post opens with a list or every post opens with a question stops surprising anyone by the third one.
