# Page template with example copy

A section-by-section template matching SKILL.md step 2, filled in for a fictional med spa, Willow Aesthetics, selling a $97 introductory consult visit. Swap in any business; keep the section order and the congruency rule (every number matches the ad).

## 1. Headline

> "A Real Consult, Not a Sales Pitch: $97 Gets You an Honest Answer on {area_of_interest}"

Matches the ad's exact hook and price.

## 2. Sub-headline or scarcity line (only if true and dated)

> "Currently booking consults through {date}."

Omit entirely if there's no real, dated reason for urgency.

## 3. Visual proof

A real photo of the provider and the space. Before/after photos belong here and in the in-person consult, never in the ad, and only with documented, HIPAA-compliant consent.

## 4. What you get (3 to 5 bullets, concrete)

- A 30-minute one-on-one with {provider_name}, {credential}
- A written plan you keep, whether or not you move forward
- Honest input on whether {area_of_interest} is even worth treating right now
- No pressure to book anything else that day

## 5. The form

Fields: first name, email, phone, consent checkbox. Nothing clinical here; that's handled in the practice's own intake.

> "Submitting this form sends a request for a consult. We'll confirm your exact time once it's accepted, usually within one business day."

States plainly that this is a request, not a confirmed appointment.

## 6. Credibility

- Named provider with credential, photo, and a one-line bio
- Testimonials pulled only from the proof ledger, with permission scope respected (named vs. anonymized)
- A comparison table using the exact same numbers as the ad, if the offer is being compared to a "typical" price

## 7. How it works

1. Submit the form
2. Get a confirmed time (not just a request) by text or email
3. Come in for the 30-minute consult
4. Walk out with a written plan, treatment optional

## 8. FAQ (pre-answer the 2 to 3 real objections)

- "Is this a real consult or just a sales pitch?": answer honestly, state plainly that treatment is separate and not required
- "What if I decide not to do anything?": state that the visit and its plan are still theirs to keep
- "Is the $97 refundable?": state the actual, written policy, matching how the practice operates it (credited toward treatment, or not)

## 9. Footer

Required disclaimer language, contact info, and a map embed for the physical location.

## Congruency checklist before publishing

- [ ] Price on the page matches the ad exactly
- [ ] "Treatment separate, not guaranteed" language matches what the ad and the in-person script both say
- [ ] Deposit/credit wording matches the practice's actual, written policy
- [ ] No undated urgency language anywhere on the page
