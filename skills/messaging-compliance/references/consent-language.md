# Consent language variants

Not legal advice; a starting point for a business's attorney to review. Fictional businesses: Clearhead Coaching (business coach), Ridgeline HVAC (home services), Willow Aesthetics (med spa).

## Lead form checkbox (unchecked by default)

Every checkbox below carries the same required elements: the business is named, the message types are named, automated technology is disclosed, frequency varies, message and data rates may apply, STOP/HELP is named, consent is not a condition of purchase (10DLC campaign approval checks for this line specifically), and a privacy policy/terms link is given.

**Clearhead Coaching**
> "I agree to receive text messages from Clearhead Coaching, including appointment reminders and promotional offers, sent using automated technology, at the number provided. Msg frequency varies. Msg & data rates may apply. Reply STOP to opt out, HELP for help. Consent is not a condition of purchase. See our [Privacy Policy]({privacy_policy_link}) and [Terms]({terms_link})."

**Ridgeline HVAC**
> "I agree to receive text messages from Ridgeline HVAC, including service updates, scheduling, and promotional offers, sent using automated technology, at the number provided. Msg frequency varies. Msg & data rates may apply. Reply STOP to opt out, HELP for help. Consent is not a condition of purchase. See our [Privacy Policy]({privacy_policy_link}) and [Terms]({terms_link})."

**Willow Aesthetics**
> "I agree to receive text messages from Willow Aesthetics, including consult reminders and promotional offers, sent using automated technology, at the number provided. Msg frequency varies. Msg & data rates may apply. Reply STOP to opt out, HELP for help. Consent is not a condition of purchase. See our [Privacy Policy]({privacy_policy_link}) and [Terms]({terms_link})."

## Point-of-sale or in-person verbal disclosure (before capturing a number for texting)

A verbal disclosure like this only covers appointment reminders and other service messages, never marketing texts. Marketing requires the written consent captured through the checkbox above (or an equivalent signed, written form); asking for a number verbally and then sending promotional texts off that alone is not compliant.

> "Can I grab your number to text you a confirmation and a reminder or two about your appointment? You can opt out anytime by texting STOP."

Record that this was said and when, and what it covered (service messages only). A verbal disclosure without any record is hard to defend if ever challenged, and recording "service messages only" matters if the business later wants to add marketing texts for the same contact, since that needs separate written consent.

## HELP reply template

> "{business_name} support: reply with your question or call us at {support_phone}. Reply STOP to opt out."

## STOP confirmation (automatic, every platform should send this)

> "You're unsubscribed from {business_name} texts and won't receive further messages. Reply START to opt back in."

## What these examples don't cover

- Any AI-voiced or autodialed marketing call needs its own prior express written consent, captured separately from a texting-only checkbox; don't assume one consent covers both channels unless the language explicitly names both.
- A purchased or third-party lead list's consent language is not shown here; see messaging-compliance SKILL.md step 6 on verifying the one-to-one consent rule's current status before relying on that kind of consent at all.
