# YouTube video plan

You'll finish with a one-page plan ready to film from: 3 title options, a thumbnail concept, a written first-15-seconds hook, a full section outline, and retention checkpoints placed where attention typically drops.

## Before you start

Read `.agents/business-context.md` and `.agents/proof-ledger.md` if they exist. If business-context is missing, offer to run **business-context** first, or ask only what's missing:

1. What's the core promise of this video in one sentence? (If you can't say it in one sentence, the video doesn't have a plan yet, it has a topic.)
2. Who is this video for, specifically? (Not "business owners," but "the business owner who just hit $500K revenue and can't keep up with leads.")
3. Roughly how long should it run, and what's the channel's normal video length?

## How to do it

### 1. Research before writing a single title

Pull up YouTube search for the topic and the channel's own past videos before drafting anything:
- **Search and suggested results**: what titles and thumbnails are already ranking for this topic or question? Note the common angle (curiosity, benefit, number) so this video either fits a proven pattern or deliberately breaks it, not by accident.
- **This channel's own retention graphs**, if past videos exist: open Studio analytics for the 2 to 3 most-watched past videos and note where the audience-retention line drops hardest. That drop point is almost always either a slow intro, a section that didn't deliver fast enough, or a tone shift. Plan this video's pacing to avoid repeating the same drop.
- **Past titles/thumbnails that under- or over-performed relative to the channel's average**: use **outlier-research** if a fuller pull is needed.

### 2. Separate the title from the thumbnail

They do different jobs. The title gives context inside search and suggested feeds; the thumbnail stops the scroll in a crowded grid where most viewers have no context yet. Write them separately, then check they don't repeat the same words (title and thumbnail text repeating each other wastes one of the two slots).

Write 3 title options, each testing a different angle:
- **Curiosity angle**: withholds the payoff
- **Benefit angle**: states the outcome plainly
- **Specific-number angle**: leads with a real figure or count

Thumbnail concept: 1 to 4 words max on the thumbnail, a face with a clear expression if the format uses one, high contrast against the channel's normal thumbnail style so it doesn't blend in with the rest of the feed.

### 3. Write the first 15 seconds exactly, word for word

This is the highest-leverage 15 seconds in the whole video. Use **hook-writer** to generate and score the opener, then write out what gets said, not just the idea. Confirm the hook delivers on the title/thumbnail's promise immediately; a hook that stalls before confirming the promise loses viewers who came in skeptical.

### 4. Build the section outline, and name the chapters

| Section | Job | Typical length |
|---|---|---|
| Hook | Confirm the promise, create the open loop | 0:00 to 0:15 |
| Setup | Why this matters, who it's for | 0:15 to 1:00 |
| Payoff 1 | First concrete piece of value | varies |
| Retention checkpoint | Re-open a loop or tease what's ahead | placed before the drop-off point found in step 1, or before the halfway mark if no past data exists |
| Payoff 2, 3... | Remaining concrete value, in ascending or logical order | varies |
| Close | Land the promise, one clear next step | final 30 to 60 seconds |

Order payoffs so the strongest one isn't buried last; viewers who drop off early should still get real value, not just setup. Write a chapter title for each section (the setup and each payoff, at minimum) short enough to read in the progress bar; chapters double as a scannable table of contents and give the viewer a reason to jump back in after a drop.

### 5. Place retention checkpoints where attention drops

Default checkpoints, used only when there's no retention graph from step 1 to go on: right after the hook (did it deliver?), roughly a quarter of the way through as a starting point, not a fixed rule, and right before any section that requires more attention than the one before it. When a real retention graph exists, place the checkpoint just ahead of wherever that specific channel's past videos lost people. At each checkpoint, re-open curiosity: name what's coming, reference back to the hook's promise, or drop a new specific detail.

### 6. Plan the thumbnail test, not just the thumbnail

Where the platform's testing tools are available, plan 2 to 3 thumbnail variants to test against each other rather than shipping one guess. Note which element is being tested (the text, the expression, the color) so a result teaches something instead of changing everything at once.

### 7. Write the description and first comment

- Description: 1 to 2 sentences restating the promise, no stuffed keyword lists.
- Pin a first comment that extends the conversation with a direct question tied to the video's core idea, to seed comments early.

## Check it

- [ ] Past retention data (or search/suggested results, if no past videos exist) was checked before drafting titles
- [ ] All 3 titles pass a plain-language test: would a stranger understand what they'd get from watching?
- [ ] Thumbnail text and title text don't repeat the same words
- [ ] The hook is written word for word, not summarized as an idea
- [ ] The hook delivers on the title/thumbnail promise within the first sentence
- [ ] At least one retention checkpoint appears before the halfway point
- [ ] Each major section has a named chapter
- [ ] The close names one clear next action, not three

## Rules

- Never promise something in the title/thumbnail that the video doesn't deliver. That's a fast way to lose channel trust and watch time.
- Don't put the strongest payoff last "to keep them watching." Viewers who drop early should still get real value.
- Any stat, number, or claim in the hook or outline traces to the proof ledger or ships as `[NEEDS PROOF: ...]`.
