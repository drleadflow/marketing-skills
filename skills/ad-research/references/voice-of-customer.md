# Finding real customer language

Five sources, in order of effort-to-yield:

| Source | Yield | Effort | What only it gives you |
|---|---|---|---|
| Call recordings / voice notes | very high | high | the final objection right before they bought |
| Support messages, no-shows, refund reasons | high | low | failure language, and angles to avoid even if they clicked well |
| Reviews | high | lowest | the exact nouns the buyer uses |
| Post-purchase survey | medium | low | real-time thinking at the moment of purchase |
| Reddit, forums, groups | medium | medium | the language of people who have not bought yet |

Two things worth doing on every project:

- **Two and three star reviews are the objection bank.** "It's fine but..." is a reservation, and
  reservations are the objections your copy needs to pre-answer.
- **Use AI for retrieval, never invention.** Asking a model "what objections might a buyer have"
  returns generic guesses (price, trust, time). Asking it to search and quote from a pile of real
  reviews or transcripts returns the actual words. Only the second use is research.

## Example: translating the marketer's word into the buyer's word

| Category | Marketer says | Buyer says |
|---|---|---|
| Hair | thinning, hair loss | "I can see my scalp," "hair in the drain" |
| Skin | tightening, rejuvenation | "I look tired even when I'm not" |
| Weight | GLP-1 side effects | "I lost the weight but the skin stayed" |
| Pain | joint support | "I can't get off the floor without help" |

Every marketer word asks the reader to translate into their own feeling first. A headline in the
buyer's own word skips that step, which is most of why it stops the scroll.

## Fictional example

Fictional business "Riverstone Dental" collecting voice of customer for a same-day-crown offer:
- Accommodation: "I started chewing on one side so it wouldn't hurt."
- Failed solution: "Tried the mail-order night guard first, it did nothing for the actual tooth."
- Nearly didn't book: "Everywhere else wanted two visits two weeks apart and I travel for work."
- The word: "cracked," not "fractured." "Fixed today," not "restored."
