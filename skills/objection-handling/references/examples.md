# Worked examples: all 8 objections, two fictional businesses

Business coach: Avery Lane Coaching (fictional), $1,000/month coaching offer. Home-services company: Lindqvist Heating & Air (fictional), a full-system-replacement consult.

## Objection table format

Use this table as the deliverable for a real client, one row per objection, filled from their actual language and their actual proof ledger:

| Objection (their words) | Real concern (likely) | Response script | Proof used | Fallback if unresolved |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| "It's too expensive." | Unsure it's worth it, not just the number | See script 1 below | P-xxx or [NEEDS PROOF] | Smaller approved starting option, or "not yet" |

## 1. "It's too expensive."

**Business coach**
> "I hear you, $1,000 a month is a real commitment. Can I ask, is it more that the number itself is above what you budgeted, or that you're not sure yet it's worth it?"
> Prospect: "Honestly, not sure it's worth it yet."
> "Totally fair. What would make it obviously worth it to you, three months in?"
> Proof used: none (routes to a specific, named outcome tied to their stated priority from discovery, not a ledger claim).

**Home services**
> "That's a real number for a full system, I get it. Is it more that it's above what you budgeted, or that you're weighing it against just patching the old one again?"
> Prospect: "Bit of both, but mostly just want to know it's worth it this time."
> "Fair. On the budget side, let's look at financing options. On the worth-it side, here's exactly what the diagnostic found and why patching it again won't hold." Proof used: none (routes to the diagnostic findings, not a ledger claim).

## 2. "I'm not sure this will work for me."

**Business coach**
> "That's a fair thing to want to know before you commit. What's making you unsure, is it something about your specific situation, or more that you've tried coaching before and it didn't stick?"
> Prospect: "Tried a group program once, didn't finish it."
> "That's useful to know. This isn't a group program, it's one-on-one with a weekly call, so the thing that usually breaks a group program isn't in play here." Proof used: none.

**Home services**
> "Makes sense to want certainty on that. Is it the brand, the install, or something about your specific house?"
> Prospect: "Just worried it's another band-aid."
> "That's exactly what the diagnostic visit rules out before you spend anything on a full system." Proof used: none.

## 3. "I need to think about it."

**Business coach**
> "Of course. Before you go, is there something specific still unresolved, or is it more wanting to sit with it?"
> Prospect: "I guess I'm not sure I'll follow through."
> "That's a real concern, and a fair one. What's made that hard in the past?"
> [Surfaces the real blocker, follow-through history, which the coach can address with an accountability structure, not a price change.]

**Home services**
> "Of course, this isn't a decision to rush. Is there something specific still unresolved, like a question about the system itself, or is it more wanting time to sit with it?"
> Prospect: "Just want to get a second quote."
> "Totally reasonable. When you do, ask them for the same diagnostic breakdown we gave you, load calculation and SEER2 rating included, so you're comparing the same thing."

## 4. "I need to talk to my spouse/partner."

**Business coach**
> "Makes total sense, that's worth including them in. Is there anything about this you think they'd push back on, so we can think it through together now?"
> Prospect: "Probably the monthly commitment."
> "Want me to put together a one-page summary you can show them tonight, so you're not trying to explain it from memory?"

**Home services**
> "Of course. Is there anything specific about the plan you think they'd want more detail on?"
> Prospect: "Probably just the total cost."
> "I'll email the full written quote right now so you both have the exact numbers in front of you."

## 5. "I've tried something like this before and it didn't work."

**Business coach**
> "That's frustrating, and it makes sense you'd be cautious. What did you try, and what do you think went wrong?"
> Prospect: "Bought a course, never opened it after week one."
> "That tracks. A course has no one checking in on you. This is a weekly call where someone notices if you stall, which is the specific piece that was missing."

**Home services**
> "That's frustrating. What was done last time, and did it get fixed or just patched?"
> Prospect: "They topped off refrigerant twice, never looked deeper."
> "That's a patch, not a diagnosis. This visit is specifically the deeper look that didn't happen last time."

## 6. "I can get this cheaper somewhere else."

**Business coach**
> "You might be able to. Can I ask what that program includes, so we're comparing the same thing?"
> Prospect: "Looks like just prerecorded videos."
> "That's a different product than what we're talking about. This is live, one-on-one, with a call every week. Worth comparing on what's included, not just the number."

**Home services**
> "You might find a lower quote. Can I ask what that quote includes, labor warranty length and all?"
> Prospect: "Not sure, didn't ask."
> "Worth calling back and asking. A lot of the lowest quotes are missing a real labor warranty, which is where the cost shows up later."

## 7. "Now isn't the right time."

**Business coach**
> "Timing matters. What would need to change for the timing to be right?"
> Prospect: "Just slammed until next quarter."
> "Makes sense. Want me to hold your spot and check back in 6 weeks, rather than starting cold from scratch then?"

**Home services**
> "I get that. What would need to change for the timing to work?"
> Prospect: "Honestly just waiting for a bigger paycheck."
> "Totally fair. The diagnostic itself is low-cost and tells you exactly what you're working with, so when the timing's right you're not starting from zero."

## 8. "I don't think I need this."

**Business coach**
> "Fair, worth being sure about. What would need to be true for you to feel like this was necessary right now?"
> Prospect: "Guess if my current approach stops working completely."
> "That's useful. Earlier you mentioned [their stated priority from discovery] mattered to you. Does waiting for it to fully break first still serve that, or does it cost you more the longer it goes?"

**Home services**
> "Fair, worth being sure. What would need to be true for this to feel necessary?"
> Prospect: "If it stops cooling completely, I guess."
> "That's useful to know. You mentioned your kid's room is already unbearable by afternoon. Is waiting for full failure still the right call given that, or is this already costing you something?"
