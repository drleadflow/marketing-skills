# Content pillars

You'll finish with 3 to 5 named content pillars, a one-line job for each, and a 4-week calendar that tells the owner exactly what to post every day without reopening this decision.

## Before you start

Read `.agents/business-context.md` and `.agents/proof-ledger.md` if they exist. If `business-context.md` is missing, offer to run **business-context** first, or ask only what you cannot work without:

1. Who is the face of the brand, and what do they know that their buyer doesn't?
2. What result do you want a stranger to feel after seeing 5 posts in a row? (Trust you, want to hire you, want to learn from you, want to join your list?)
3. Which platform gets the most of your real attention right now?

## How to do it

### 1. Separate the content lane from the revenue lane

The content doesn't have to be about the thing you sell. It has to prove you're the kind of person/company that can be trusted with the thing you sell. A home-services company that sells HVAC tune-ups can post about "what contractors won't tell you" and "how we train new techs" without ever mentioning the tune-up. The offer is the proof underneath the content, not the subject of it.

Pick the lane explicitly:
- **Direct lane**: content teaches the exact thing you sell (a business coach teaching the framework they sell).
- **Adjacent lane**: content teaches the world around the thing you sell, the offer stays implicit proof (a med spa posting about skin science and aging myths rather than treatment menus).
- **Builder lane**: content shows the work of building the business itself, the audience watches you operate (a home-services owner posting about hiring, pricing, and the business side).

Name the lane before you name pillars. It decides the tone of every pillar below.

### 2. Draft 3 to 5 pillars

A pillar is not a topic, it's a repeatable angle with a job. Each pillar needs:
- **Name** (2 to 4 words, memorable enough to say out loud)
- **Job** (what it does for the funnel: builds trust, shifts a belief, proves a result, drives a specific action)
- **Belief it shifts or question it answers** (pull from real customer language if the business has call logs, DMs, or past consult notes to draw from)
- **Format it favors** (talking-head, carousel, text post, voice note, newsletter)
- **Cadence** (how often it runs)

Standard pillar shapes to adapt, not copy verbatim:

| Pillar type | Job | Example name |
|---|---|---|
| Myth-buster | Shift a belief the buyer holds that's costing them | "Actually, No" |
| Behind the build | Prove competence by showing the work | "How We Build It" |
| Receipts | Prove results with a specific number or before/after | "The Numbers" |
| Objection answer | Pre-handle the #1 reason people don't buy | "The Real Reason" |
| Identity callout | Speak to one exact person so they feel seen | "If You're [X]" |

A coach, a home-services owner, and a med spa owner will fill these differently, but the five jobs above cover almost any business. Don't invent a sixth pillar unless one of these five doesn't fit.

### 3. Weight the pillars to the funnel stage you're weakest at

If attention is the chokepoint, weight myth-buster and identity callout heavier (they travel). If appointments or closing is the chokepoint, weight objection answer and receipts heavier (they convert warm traffic). Ask which chokepoint hurts most right now before fixing weights.

### 4. Build the 4-week calendar

One row per planned post. Rotate pillars so no single pillar runs twice in a row.

| Week | Mon | Tue | Wed | Thu | Fri |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Myth-buster | Behind the build | Receipts | Objection answer | Identity callout |
| 2 | Receipts | Myth-buster | Identity callout | Behind the build | Objection answer |
| 3 | Objection answer | Receipts | Myth-buster | Identity callout | Behind the build |
| 4 | Identity callout | Objection answer | Behind the build | Receipts | Myth-buster |

Adjust the number of slots to the owner's real posting capacity. Five days a week for a solo operator is false confidence; most people can sustain 2 to 3.

### 5. Hand each slot to a format skill

For each calendar slot, write the one-line topic and route to the skill that writes it: **hook-writer** for the opening line, **short-form-script** for talking-head video, **carousel-writer** for a carousel, **newsletter** for email, **seo-article** for a blog post.

## Check it

- [ ] 3 to 5 pillars, each with a name, a job, and a format
- [ ] No pillar is just "topic" (e.g. "skincare") without a belief or a job attached
- [ ] The calendar has no two identical pillars back to back
- [ ] The weighting matches the funnel stage the owner said is weakest
- [ ] Every claim used as an example traces to the proof ledger or is marked `[NEEDS PROOF: ...]`
- [ ] If any pillar example cites an approved claim, the deliverable lists `Proof used: P-xxx` for it

## Rules

- Never invent a number, result, or client story to fill a pillar example. Use `[NEEDS PROOF: ...]` and move on.
- Don't build more than 5 pillars. More than 5 means the owner can't remember their own strategy, which defeats the point.
- Health, money, legal, and before/after claims in any pillar example follow **health-claims-check** when the business is health-related.
