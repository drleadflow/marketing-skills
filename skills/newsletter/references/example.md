# Worked example: single-idea newsletter issue (fictional home-services company)

Business: Lindqvist Heating & Air (fictional). List: past customers and lead-magnet subscribers. One idea: why skipping one maintenance visit costs more than the visit itself.

**Subject**: "The maintenance call we see skipped every spring"
**Preview text**: "It's not the filter. It's the gap."

**Body**:

"Someone called us this week asking why their system failed in the first heat wave of the year.

It wasn't one thing. They skipped last fall's tune-up because the system "seemed fine," then skipped this spring's too, because nothing was obviously wrong yet.

A system that's checked twice a year catches small wear before it becomes a breakdown. A system that's checked once every year and a half doesn't get caught early, it gets caught in July, at the worst possible time to wait on a part.

If you've fallen behind on a maintenance schedule, the fix isn't a better system. It's getting back on a schedule you'll keep, even if that means fall-only instead of fall and spring, rather than no schedule at all.

If you want a second look at where your system stands, reply and tell me when it was last checked. I'll tell you honestly whether it's due."

**CTA**: "Reply and tell me when it was last checked."

Proof used: none (no approved ledger claim used; the maintenance-cadence point is a general mechanism, not a specific statistic).
