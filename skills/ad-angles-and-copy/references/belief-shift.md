# The belief-shift pass for ads

Use this when the offer involves real trust, money, or vulnerability (health, high-ticket
services, big life changes) and a plain angle bank is not moving bookings even though the hooks
stop the scroll. Time: about an hour from what you already know, two hours if you interview the
owner or provider. This is the ad-specific application of **positioning**'s six beliefs; it does
not define a second belief list. If `.agents/business-context.md` already has the business's
belief table filled in from **positioning**, start there instead of rebuilding it.

## Inputs

Business context, the customer-language bank from `ad-research`, the owner or provider's own
words if available (a transcript, reviews, a short voice note on why they work the way they do),
and **positioning**'s six-belief table if it already exists for this business.

## Steps

1. **Avatar and villain.** The avatar is built from the customer's actual sentences, not a
   generic persona. The villain is a business model or broken system ("the discount clinic that
   never follows up"), never a named competitor.
2. **Two identities.** Name the before (specific and a little embarrassing, something the
   customer recognizes in themselves) and the after (specific and proud). Keep both short enough
   to repeat.
3. **Positioning's six beliefs, not a new list.** In this exact order: (1) the problem is real,
   and deeper than the symptom they see, (2) this method is the fastest path to what they want,
   (3) now is the time, waiting costs more than acting, (4) it's worth the money, a return not a
   cost, (5) it beats the other options they're weighing (competitors, doing it themselves, doing
   nothing), (6) they don't have to do it alone, a guide gets them there faster. Use positioning's
   written row for each: their current belief in their own words, the new belief, and the proof
   you have. If the proof is missing, write the row as a placeholder, not a claim.
4. **Paid kit.** From the six rows: 4 hooks (each tagged to the belief it answers), 3 bodies, 2
   CTAs, and a 6-cell test matrix.
5. **Turn each belief into one static headline**, built in two different visual treatments so you
   are testing the belief, not the layout.

## Gate

- Every hook speaks to a situation or group, never "you" plus a personal attribute (see
  **ad-policy-check**'s personal-attributes rule).
- Zero em dashes.
- No invented stats or stories.
- Every placeholder marked `[NEEDS PROOF: ...]`, never silently filled in.
- The six beliefs used here are the same six as in `.agents/business-context.md`'s positioning
  section; this file is not a second, divergent version of that table.

## Fictional worked example

Fictional business "Cedarbrook Family Chiropractic," belief 4 (the price is a return, not a
cost):
- Current belief (their words): "Chiropractic is a slow, expensive maybe."
- New belief: "One focused exam tells you exactly what's wrong and what fixes it, same
  visit."
- Proof on hand: `[NEEDS PROOF: average number of visits to resolution, from our own records]`
- Headline: "You don't need six weeks of guessing. You need one real exam."
