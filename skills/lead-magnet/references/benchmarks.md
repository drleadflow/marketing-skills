# Benchmarks

Reference ranges for judging whether a lead magnet's numbers are off, or just normal for its format and source. Treat every row as a starting point to calibrate against your own data, not a target to hit on day one.

## Conversion rate by format

| Format | Typical landing-page conversion |
|---|---|
| Checklist | 30 to 50 percent |
| Cheat sheet | 25 to 40 percent |
| Template | 25 to 45 percent |
| Short guide | 20 to 35 percent |
| Quiz | 30 to 50 percent |
| Webinar registration | 20 to 40 percent (then 30 to 50 percent show up) |
| Mini-course | 15 to 30 percent |

## Conversion rate by traffic source

| Source | Typical conversion |
|---|---|
| Content upgrade inside a blog post | 3 to 8 percent of readers |
| Dedicated page, organic or referral traffic | 20 to 40 percent |
| Dedicated page, paid traffic | 10 to 25 percent |
| Comment-to-DM on social | 10 to 20 percent of those who DM in |
| Exit-intent popup | 2 to 5 percent of visitors |

## What a quality lead looks like downstream

- Opens the first few follow-up messages at a normal or above-normal rate for the channel
- Progresses to a booked call within 90 days at roughly the same rate as the account's other lead sources
- Doesn't unsubscribe or opt out within the first few touches

If a lead magnet pulls high volume but those downstream numbers lag the account's average, the format attracted the wrong stage of buyer, not a tracking problem. Narrow the topic or tighten the gate before blaming the follow-up sequence.
